    Build Trail→ 0.3 mi

    7 checkpoints to a fast car

    Checkpoint 1 of 7

    Sketch it first

    Draw your idea on paper, cut it out and stick it to the block. The simplest aerodynamic shape is a wedge. Skip the super-pointy nose so your car rests against the starting pin, and make the front and back easy to tell apart.

    • Download free design templates
    • See hundreds of reader cars

    Checkpoint 2 of 7

    Rough cut the shape

    Use a coping saw, or ask a responsible adult to help with a power tool. Leave plenty of wood at the back of the car so you have room to add weight there later.

    • How to make a fast Pinewood Derby car

    Checkpoint 3 of 7

    Smooth it, then shine it

    Wrap sandpaper around a small block of wood and work from coarse to medium to fine grit. Seal the grain, then paint with long, smooth strokes (no dabbing!). Start a week or two before race day.

    • Paint a glossy finish

    Checkpoint 4 of 7

    Max it out

    Make your car as heavy as the rules allow, usually 5 ounces. Science says the heaviest spot should be about 1 inch in front of the rear axle. Too far back and your car may pop a wheelie!

    • Where do the weights go?
    • Speed science from a NASA engineer

    Checkpoint 5 of 7

    Straight and smooth

    Pick the four straightest axles (bent ones wobble when an adult spins them in a drill), file off burrs, then polish. Glue the axles in straight: your car should roll in a line across a smooth floor.

    • Polish your axles and wheels

    Checkpoint 6 of 7

    Fight friction

    Add graphite or another dry lubricant to the wheels and axles. Less friction between the body and wheels means more speed down the track.

    • 10 steps to a fast car
    • Use science to go faster

    Checkpoint 7 of 7

    Check in and have fun

    Know your pack’s rules before you build, bring your car to check-in on weight, and cheer for every car. The most important rule of the Pinewood Derby? Have fun!
